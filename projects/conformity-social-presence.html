Quantifying the Effect of Social Presence on Online Social Conformity

How does the perceived social presence of an online quizzing environment shape conformity behaviour? This study isolates three design levers — interactivity, response visibility, and user representation — and measures each one's effect on whether people change their answers to match the group.

Background

Social presence — the perceived availability of others in a mediated environment — is a long-studied construct in face-to-face conformity research. But online platforms vary enormously in how much social presence they manufacture: some show real-time discussion, some hide identities, some replace people with generic avatars. Whether and how those design choices change conformity behaviour was an open question.

Approach

We manipulated social presence along three axes in a controlled online quizzing experiment:

  • Interactivity — discussion vs. no discussion among group members.
  • Response visibility — public vs. private answers.
  • User representation — generic avatars vs. user-specific avatars.

The "Confederates' App" simulated group discussions between four confederates and one real participant, allowing us to control the social context that the participant experienced.

Findings

  • Conformity was highest in the presence of peer discussions and public responses.
  • Conformity was lowest when discussion was absent and responses were private.
  • Higher social presence consistently led to higher online conformity, mirroring patterns from face-to-face research.
